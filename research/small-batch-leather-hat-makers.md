## Bottom line

> **Verdict:** Facopel is real but sits 50 km outside Florence and is a 165,000-hats-a-year luxury-brand supplier, so a high minimum makes sense. I could not find "Exo Hats" or "A B by Afruz" anywhere online; Carl needs to send their Instagram handles. The best route is to split the work: a small sample room or leather cap workshop (Nelson Were, Owen Barry, C & M Hatters) assembles, Darian's Italian tanneries supply leather, and a jeweller makes the magnetic peak piece.

## The three makers Carl found

### 1. Facopel: confirmed, with corrections

- **Real name:** Facopel (Facopel Produzione), part of Gruppo Florence S.p.A.
- **Location:** Viale della Lama 1/A, Chiesina Uzzanese, Pistoia, Tuscany. About 50 km west of Florence, not in the city
- **Website:** https://facopel.gruppoflorence.it/en/
- **What they make:** Luxury hats since 1908, including leather and fur since the 1970s, for 40+ Italian and international fashion houses. Own brands: Luca Della Lama and Lula
- **Size:** 47 staff, 5,500 m² of production space and 165,000 items a year (company page); a 2022 group article says 54 staff
- **Minimums:** Not published. Carl's 50 per colour and no one-offs came from talking to them and is plausible for a supplier this size, but I could not confirm it in writing
- **Contact:** +39 0572 48702 (contacts page)

### 2. Exo Hats (Armenia): not found

Searches in English for Exo Hats, Exohats and Armenian crocodile hat makers found no business by that name. Every "EXO hats" result was K-pop merchandise. No Armenian maker of crocodile hats showed up either. (See the follow-up section below for the re-check under the spelling "Ekzo".)

- Closest Armenian hat maker: Nshanyan (Armenian By Origin), Yerevan, handmade headwear since 2013, but no exotic leather (https://nshanyanhats.com/en).
- A search summary named RAF Kanian, a Yerevan brand working in crocodile, python and ostrich, but only for shoes, bags and jackets. I could not open its page, so it is unconfirmed.
- The name may be a mishearing, or the maker may only be on Instagram. Ask Carl for the handle or a screenshot.

### 3. A B by Afruz (Iran): not found

I searched Afruz, Afrouz and Afrooz with hat, leather and milliner. No woman hat maker by that name came up.

- The nearest name match is Afrouz Leather, an Iranian tannery and exporter of cow and camel leather (afrouzleather.com, per search results; the site did not load). It is a materials supplier, not a hat maker, so it is probably not her.
- Other Iranian custom hat makers found: Sample Hat in Tehran, about 50 years old and taking custom orders by Instagram message; Carlitos (https://carlitos.ir/), fully custom handmade hats including leather; MAD Leathers (https://madleathers.ir/), which sells leather caps and gloves.
- Tehran-born milliner Maryam Keyhani works in Berlin and makes sculptural art hats (https://www.anothermag.com/art-photography/14885/milliner-maryam-keyhani). She is not a match for Afruz.
- Ask Carl for the exact Instagram handle. A one-woman maker who only posts on Instagram would not show up in web search.

## Business models, ranked

### 1. Split the build across specialists

- **How it works:** A pattern-maker or sample room perfects the cap. Darian's Italian tanneries supply the leather, and his existing ratchet hardware goes in. A jeweller makes the magnetic peak pieces, and one small cap workshop does final assembly.
- **Pros:** No factory minimum. Darian keeps the IP, materials and quality control. Each part goes to the best person for it, and the jewellery tier ($600 up to $100,000) is separate from the cap body.
- **Cons:** Darian or Carl has to coordinate it, check quality at every handover and keep the specs tight. If the pattern is wrong, the error carries through every unit.
- **Cost and time:** C & M Hatters and Albrizio take 1 to 10 prototypes; Owen Barry costs depend on how much pattern work you bring.
- **Who could do it:** Samples: C & M Hatters or Albrizio (New York), Angiolo Frasconi (Florence). Assembly: Owen Barry or Nelson Were (UK). Exotic skins: Tan Sin or JewelPelle (Thailand).

### 2. Small-run leather cap workshops (10 to 50 units)

- **Pros:** Fits the 50 to 100 unit plan, and these workshops already make caps in leather. The 50 investor units could be remade properly at this scale.
- **Cons:** They use their own patterns and processes unless you supply yours. Exotics and jewellery fittings may be new to them.
- **Cost and time:** Owen Barry: 10 units per style per colour. C & M Hatters and Albrizio: 10 to 50 production. Knickerbocker: 25 to 200. Szoneier: 1 unit first order.
- **Who could do it:** Owen Barry, Nelson Were (manufacturing service), C & M Hatters, Albrizio, Knickerbocker Mfg., Deri Sitesi (Istanbul).

### 3. Bespoke one-off atelier for the top tier

- **Pros:** Best quality, true one-offs for buyers at $10,000 and up, and short lead times.
- **Cons:** High cost per unit, it does not scale, and the maker may want visible credit.
- **Cost and time:** OJ Exclusive from €795, 4 to 6 weeks; Nelson Were £185; Ries Seoul 2 to 3 weeks; Triangles Hat 3 to 4 weeks; Super Duper 72 hours.
- **Who could do it:** OJ Exclusive, Nelson Were, Jill Corbett, Ries Seoul, Triangles Hat, Bobby Art Leather, Super Duper Hats.

### 4. Exotic-skin OEM specialists

- **Pros:** Skins, cutting skill and CITES export papers from one supplier, and small orders are accepted.
- **Cons:** Better known for bags and belts than caps, so expect to supply the cap pattern. They are far away, so plan for shipping and sampling cycles.
- **Who could do it:** Tan Sin (own crocodile farm, small orders), JewelPelle (croc, python, stingray), Crocodile Viet (already makes croc caps), Cape Karoo (ostrich).

### 5. Italian artisan workshops through trade networks

- **Pros:** Made-in-Italy story, close to Darian's tanneries, and Tuscany has many small conto terzi (third-party) workshops.
- **Cons:** You need Italian and an introduction, and minimums vary. The Italian Artisan platform says plainly it is not for one-offs (30 to 50 per style).
- **Who could do it:** CNA Federmoda Toscana Centro (https://toscanacentro.cna.it/federmoda-2/), Angiolo Frasconi, Italian Artisan (hats from 30 pieces per style, https://italianartisan.com/hat-manufacturer/), and Darian's tannery contacts for referrals.

### 6. Co-brand or license with an artisan who already does it

- **Pros:** Ready-made capability and credibility, plus a story for social media.
- **Cons:** Shared margin and IP risk, so Darian needs a contract covering the ratchet design.
- **Who could do it:** Hand Made Design Ainhoa Garcia (already makes jewelled leather caps), Ferrieri (gold filigree on croc caps), OJ Exclusive.

### 7. In-house assembly with trained local labour

- **Pros:** Matches Darian's plan to do everything in-house with Jess and later move to Costa Rica. Full control and the strongest brand story.
- **Cons:** Needs cap patterns, leather machines (Nelson Were lists cylinder-arm machines) and months of skill-building. Quality will be uneven at first.
- **Who could help:** Buy the finished pattern from a sample room (model 1), then train with a leather craftsman such as Jack & Rose, which runs leathercraft workshops.

### 8. Maker platforms and marketplaces

- **Pros:** Fast way to find more small-run makers, with filters for minimum order size.
- **Cons:** Mostly garment factories, few with luxury leather headwear, and you still have to vet them.
- **Who could do it:** Maker's Row (US factories, lists C & M Hatters), Sewport (escrow, minimum-order filter, https://sewport.com/how-it-works), Etsy leather cap sellers such as WolfstrykerLeather.

Made-to-order pre-sales fit alongside any of these: sell the investor-grade sample, take deposits, then place the 300 to 500 unit order. I did not find a source with costs for this, so it is not ranked.

## Follow-up: crocodile cap profit and Ekzo

> **Short answer:** There is profit in a crocodile cap, but not at $600. Making one or buying one costs about $550 to $1,000 (estimate). Comparable luxury crocodile caps sell for $2,950 to $6,750. So the crocodile cap should sit at $1,500 and up, with the magnetic jewellery carrying the $10,000+ tiers. Figures use the ECB rate of 20 Nov 2026, €1 = $1.099.

## Exotic skins: the paperwork

> **CITES:** Crocodile, python and similar skins are controlled under CITES, and each cross-border shipment needs papers. OJ Exclusive and JewelPelle both say they handle CITES documents. Check this before anything ships from Canada to Costa Rica or elsewhere.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Get the real handles for Exo Hats (Ekzo) and Afruz.** Carl or Darian sends Instagram links or screenshots, and I re-check them.
2. **Send one brief to five makers.** Nelson Were, Owen Barry, C & M Hatters, Angiolo Frasconi and Tan Sin: 1 sample plus a 50-unit quote, with Darian supplying leather and ratchets.
3. **Commission a master pattern.** Have a sample room (C & M Hatters or Albrizio) fix the ratchet back and peak magnet seat, so every maker works to the same pattern.
4. **Find a jeweller for the magnetic peak.** Treat it as its own product line, priced separately from the cap body.
5. **Ask Facopel in writing.** Request their minimum and price for samples only, so the 50-per-colour figure is on record.
6. **Sort CITES before you buy skins.** Confirm export papers for any exotic skin with the supplier.
